It tests every signal that moves money or reputation against the decisions in [02-decisions.md](02-decisions.md) as they stood on 2026-10-06. ## 1. The cheapest attack: a ring that co-signs itself

This walk-through uses only DECIDED rows.

1. Four friends form a ring. Each takes a turn as host and picks a pattern. The other three are players and send Positive Gossip. That makes the session co-signed (D30: at least 3 players send Positive Gossip). The host reaches Maturity on that pattern (D28, D29: "hosted others in it").
2. Recording needs no typing, only picking cards from a list (D124). Nothing sets a minimum length or requires the session to be announced, so a session can take minutes.
3. After 8 such sessions each host is a Cautious Optimist (D23). That is 32 sessions for the whole ring. Each member can then buy a €100 certificate (D69, D162). The certificate lists the same three witnesses every time (D160), and that is the only thing that gives the ring away.
4. All their Positive Gossip is co-signed, so all of it counts as flow-funding appreciation (D80). Flow funding pays in proportion to signals (D83). The more sessions the ring holds, the larger its share of the pool, which other people's certificate fees filled.
5. After 32 patterns each (128 sessions), all four are Maestro Minimalists. If they register three tiny user groups among themselves, they can sign equinox grants of 6% of the pool or more (D164). New groups join by open invitation with no vote (D136). Nothing stops a grant going to the signers' own new "practice space".

The ring pays €400 in certificate fees and spends time. In return it gets certificates, a share of every equinox payout, and grant signing power. **Today the only defences are visibility and after-the-fact measures**: the certificate shows its witnesses (D160), flows are published (D95), and the top-10% capture measure is judged at the equinox (D91, D171). The draft-3 line Javeline kept already says this honestly: witness diversity "shows collusion, does not prevent it" (response, L295).

## 3. How "pay for contribution, never for recruitment" is enforced

**The rule (DECIDED, D168):** payment follows use of a contribution in a co-signed session (D50), never the number of people adopted, taught, brought in or appreciated. Home-deck adoption (D18) and "learned from" lineage (D28, D114) count for recognition and phase only.

**What it allows:** a deck author earns each time their deck is *used* in a co-signed session (D18), not when someone *adopts* it. Bringing in hosts who use your deck therefore pays indirectly, through use. D168 accepts that as payment for contribution. Without G5 and G1, a ring can produce that use itself.

**Where it is checked today:** nowhere specific. The decisions name the rule but not the check. The nearest pieces are:
- flows are published and their rules change only by the commons (D95);
- payouts are settled at each equinox from co-signed records (D80);
- countable evidence comes from fixed app rules, and money never depends on an AI judgment (D182);
- the pool is held under a signer threshold (D163), and the commons judges the experiment (D171).

**G15 (PROPOSAL) makes the check exact, at two points:**
1. **In the code.** The payout calculation takes a fixed, published list of inputs and nothing else: co-signed use events (after G5), co-signed appreciation within each giver's budget (G1), and the published thresholds (D81). No field exists for sign-ups, referrals, adoptions, lineage or headcount, so recruitment can't be paid even by mistake. A test fails the build if any other input reaches the payout calculation.
2. **At each equinox, before money moves.** The proposed payout and its inputs are published before the gathering (D80, D95). Any member can question a line. The signers who release the payout (D163, D166) confirm that every payout line traces to listed inputs only. While flow funding runs by hand (D138), this human check is the enforcement.

## 4. Accepted risks, stated openly

Even with every G-fix, these remain. **DECIDED 2026-10-07 (Javeline, D203):** accepted as "paint on the road" (D161), and discussed and evaluated at each equinox gathering.

- **A ring that recruits real outsiders as witnesses** passes G2–G4. That is no longer gaming: outsiders who really took part are the evidence the system wants.
- **A ring can still certify itself** (G3, DECIDED 2026-10-06: no minimum). The certificate shows how few distinct witnesses stand behind it, and buyers judge (D185, D161).
- **Collusion inside the pilot community can't be measured by topology until the trust wire ships** (D125, D173). Until then the defences are G1–G5 and people's judgment of what the certificate shows.
- **Use of tips and riffs stays a host's claim** (Mujo's point). G5 and G6 limit who can make the claim and how often it pays, but they can't make it observable.
- **"Verified member" (G2) depends on NAOMS identity.** How NAOMS verifies one person per account isn't described in any file in the folder. ASSUMPTION (needs clarification): NAOMS can support it. Owner: Mujo.
