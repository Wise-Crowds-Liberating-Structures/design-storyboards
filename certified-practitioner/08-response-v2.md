   - "The commons" is defined (D166).
   - Stewards are admitted and removed by stated rules (D159, D199).
   - The pool is held under a signer threshold (D163, D200).
   - Starting values are confirmed by the pilot group (D170).
   - The hatch has a minimum and a maximum length (D132).
   - Leaving, forking, rubric changes and content removal are defined (D177–D180).
6. **Money is made precise (feedback §04 and §07.4).**
   - The pool fills first. Flow funding starts only when the pool can cover a season of requested survival thresholds and an equinox gathering votes for it (D163, D165).
   - The flow rules are adopted by that vote, with me abstaining (D169).
   - Payment follows use, never headcount (D168).
   - No overflow during the hatch (D84).
   - Grants have signers, a cap and a conflict-of-interest rule (D164, D190).
   - Everything money-related happens at the spring and autumn equinoxes.
7. **An adversarial pass.** Four defences are decided (D183, D187, D189, D190), and two more were decided since (D184, D185). Nine are proposals (D186, D188, D191–D197). The remaining risks are accepted and reviewed at each equinox (D203).
8. **Scope.** Every feature has a priority, a pilot and an add-back condition (see "The minimum: two pilots"). The home deck and archive rank as Priority 0 (D175). The session package for agents and Data Wash are in pilot 1 (D139, D205).
9. **Smaller fixes:**
   - The licence is sourced (D61, D62, D64).
   - The certificate fee is one fee, not per charter (D69).

   - Diplomas and courses go in full to the pool (D76).

## Why we are doing this

We are building new senses for groups, so that what they learn to see, they can learn to value. That is the pitch, and everything below serves it.

The tools we think with become part of us. A hammer used well feels like an extension of the hand, because the brain's model of the body absorbs it. Meditation and mindfulness work the same way on individual thinking.

Liberating Structures take that move collective. A group using them thinks about how it wants to think, together. We call this process mindfulness. The arc runs from mechanical to mystical: first you learn the game rules, then the games become how you see and move through the world.

The structures come out of complexity science, by way of the Santa Fe Institute and narrative inquiry. Ecocycle, the Agreement–Certainty Matrix and many others turn that theory into procedure. Playing them builds a felt, intuitive sense of how complex systems behave. We practice the theory rather than only reading it.

This matters now. We face a pile-up of second-order crises, and many people feel powerless because they meet them with old cognitive tools. The cornerstone of new tools is relationships made legible. What gets perceived can be accounted for. A materialist, reductionist accounting cannot see relationships, so it cannot value them. New senses make a new accounting possible.

In the meantime, the same tools help people get real work done: genuine co-creation that includes and unleashes every voice. Certified Practitioner is how we make that practice visible, trustworthy and self-funding.

## What we are building

Certified Practitioner is a community-owned certification program: it makes practice visible, sells what that visibility is worth, and pays the people who make it valuable (D1). Certified Practitioner is also the name of the app, replacing Kitestring as the working name (D2), and the Liberating Structures community is the first place it runs (D3).

Draft 3 frames the app as a tool for my deck. That frame is too small. The deck and the app are the ingestion layer of something larger: a certification program with a knowledge commons and courses built on top of it, and money designed in from the start (D4).

We are a large organization that is not organized. Today nobody can say where the practice is active, which user groups are running, or where the knowledge sits without someone maintaining lists by hand. Certified Practitioner lets us federate into a trench-coat totem pole: enough coherence and legibility to pass as a large organization to the enterprises that buy learning, while staying a community underneath.

The stars are the user groups: the people already using these tools wherever practice happens, who will use the app to certify their practice (D5).

The goals, in priority order (D6), with my deck and archive above them all as Priority 0 (D175):

0. **My deck first, my archive in.** The app uses each practitioner's own material first.
1. **Certified practice.** Practitioners and practice sessions carry certification a community stands behind.
2. **A knowledge commons and the automatic course.** A living wiki of the structures, how they are used and remixed, with Tips, Traps and Riffs, and online courses generated from it.
3. **A marketplace.** Paid sessions and hiring, with the app offering to handle billing for those who want it.
4. **The network sees itself.** The state of the practice is always current and at everyone's fingertips, with no directory upkeep.
5. **A white-label protocol.** Other communities of practice can run the same thing, pointing at ours as the working example, with licence income feeding the common pool.

These stack like a permaculture guild: each goal feeds the others. The ranking says what we protect when they compete for build time, not the order we build in (D174).

**One principle runs through all of it: paint on the road, not crash barriers (D161).** The system guides rather than enforces. Every claim is admitted and shown with how well it is evidenced: a personal claim, an attestation, a peer co-signature, content and artifacts, provenance dates, the shape of the witness network, a community seal. Participants and the market judge. Money is the one exception: it moves only by fixed, published rules and under a threshold of signers (D182, D200).

## Priority 1: certified practice

Certification applies to two things, not one: the practitioner and the practice session (D21). Draft 3 covers the first. The second is new, and it is where most of the market value sits.

User groups already certify people tacitly. When a group has watched you host for a year, it knows what you can do. Certified Practitioner makes that judgment explicit and portable. I spent years in a user group wanting to go pro; this is the path I did not have.

### Certified practitioners

The criteria are shared by everyone; the record belongs to the practitioner.

- **One shared rubric (D22).** The Development Phases ladder, read across four dimensions: patterns in use, people reached, diversity of settings, and design goals achieved. A 16+ in Utrecht means the same as a 16+ in Rotterdam. This is a choice, and it pulls against communities setting their own bar; we make it so a certificate means the same everywhere. Only the commons can change the rubric, going forward, and every certificate keeps the version it was issued under (D179).
- **Thresholds (D23).** Despairing Cynic, Cautious Optimist, Rapturous Super-User and Maestro Minimalist, by patterns in use. Anyone beyond that is simply a maestro. The phase uses patterns in use; the other three dimensions are shown as plain counts (D25).
- **No ceiling on the deck (D26).** Every pattern card counts, and the deck is not captive to the structures in the 2026 book. Community cards are mostly versions of the core structures; a genuinely new card counts once it has been through a development cycle and is in broad use (D27). New structures can be created and added at any time.
- **Computed, not filled in (D28, D29).** Each pattern moves through its own ecocycle for each person: Gestation (never seen it), Birth (led through it), Maturity (hosted others in it), Creative Destruction (enabled someone else to host it). The app's records show which stage applies. "Patterns in use" is the count at Maturity or beyond.
- **Shown at each evidence level (D30).** The phase is computed per evidence level and shown side by side: the co-signed phase, and the phase including claims. A session is co-signed when enough of its players send Positive Gossip (D30), and only distinct, verified members count toward that (D184). Guests are witnesses on the record but don't count toward co-signing (D119). A Scribe can record evidence for a player without a phone; only people who attest themselves count (D202).

### Certificates of practice

A certificate of practice is issued from the computed record, not from anyone's opinion (D160). It states the record and its evidence profile, never competence (D162). It can be bought once the co-signed phase reaches the minimum (D162); below that, the record stays visible in the app for free. Certifying a record is always free (D32). The fee pays for the formal document, the same fee everywhere, changed only by the commons (D69).

The certificate is signed by the relevant user groups and the LS Commons (D201). Until the LS Commons says otherwise, we presume it signs; if it doesn't, the user groups sign alone, and that may be enough. Each certificate shows how many distinct people witnessed the sessions behind it, and there is no minimum: buyers judge (D185). In pilot 1, LS Go Online and the Wise Crowds Design Call count as one community, and the certificate says so (D157).

Its form is a verifiable credential plus a printable certificate, naming the witnessing relations (D70, PROPOSAL; the format is Mujo's). The refund rule is my starting position: a full refund until the certificate is issued, none after (D121, PROPOSAL until Mujo checks it against EU consumer rules and the pilot group confirms it).

### The seal: an optional endorsement

A user group's seal is now an optional endorsement shown on a certificate, for those who want the stronger claim (D160). It comes in pilot 2. Only user groups seal, through their stewards; individuals never seal alone (D31). A seal endorses a certificate, so there is no seal without one (D34). A group can decline warmly, "not yet" (D36), and the practitioner can ask for a second review by another group's stewards (D159). Each group's charter sets who seals, the quorum and which seals it trusts, within a shared floor (D112, D159). No seal is recognised across communities before the trust wire ships (D157). OGD Eval reads the practice, never the person (D113), and stewards read the pattern across evals when they seal.

### Certified practice sessions

Whether to offer paid sessions, and at what price, is up to user groups and practitioners (D42). The market decides; the session record simply carries its tier so buyers can tell what they are paying for.

| Session tier | Who runs it | What participants get | Reference price |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Community practice | Any user group member | Practice, proof of participation | D38 |
| Certified practice | A certified practitioner | Practice with an expert, attested learning | D39 |
| Immersion workshop | Certified practitioners, explicitly teaching | A structured learning path toward the next phase | D40 |

An immersion day with a full room is real revenue (D41). Today that flows only to the few practitioners with enough personal brand to fill a room. Certification lets the community's legitimacy fill rooms for practitioners who have the skill but not the brand. ## Priority 2: the knowledge commons

Every recorded session should leave something in a shared wiki, the community should decide what rises, and online courses should fall out of it automatically. The wiki becomes the new reference for the structures, and the courses are our second product line. This is pilot 2, fed by pilot 1's records (D158).

### What it holds

- **Per pattern:** Tips (what makes it come together), Traps (what to avoid, and how), and Riffs (a remix of one element for a narrow use) (D44). Traps get equal billing with Tips (D118).
- **Per string:** designs that were actually played, with their outcomes. Anyone can "play this design" from the wiki (D116).
- **Per setting:** which patterns are used for which problems, where, and where the practice is innovating.
- **The cards themselves (D45).** Three layers, shown side by side: the core Liberating Structures as published; my deck, offered as one community set among many (this is my stuff, check it out, make your own); and community riffs on either. Any practitioner can create a card, offer it through the app, and have the community evaluate it, so the deck keeps evolving (D46). I hope others make far better cards than mine, including better versions of the ones I made. Other published decks, such as Holisticon's, could join the same way later. The app needs an affordance for offering a card as a revision of an existing one, with versions, so people can see the alternatives, choose between them, and customize their own deck (D47).

### How it is sorted

Like Reddit: up and down votes, so the cream rises (D48). Ostrom's principles are the governance frame: the community can see what is in the commons and rate it, so it governs itself.

Votes rank the wiki but never pay (D49). Votes are shown at each voter's evidence level (D151), and only members with a co-signed record vote on the course and on cards (D193, PROPOSAL). Recognition money follows use: an entry earns when it is used in a co-signed session, which is far harder to fake than a vote (D50). An entry doesn't earn when its author was in the session, and earns at most once per host per season (D187). Only the host records which entries were used (D188, PROPOSAL).

Nothing is removed for being weak; it sinks and stays visible at its rating. Removal is only for legal or safety reasons, by stewards of different groups, in the open, and reversible (D180).

A new card is accepted by community evaluation in the wiki, with versions; I vote as an ordinary member (D151). The acceptance threshold is the commons' to set (OPEN).

### How entries get in

A new step, Data Wash, comes after the session closes. Each contributor chooses, per entry, to publish it under their name, publish it anonymized, or withhold it (D51). It applies from the first data in, so it is in pilot 1 (D205). This is build item 16, a filter and a feed over data that items 1–5 already produce (D52, PROPOSAL; sizing is Mujo's).

The closing order becomes: traces, then RoTI, then Positive Gossip, then Data Wash (D53). Evidence comes first, then its evaluation, then the co-signature, then the choice to publish.

### The wiki-to-course pipeline

The pipeline is in scope, and its automation is the point: courses are a byproduct, not a separate production effort (D54).

- **The automatic course (D55, D60).** One official Certified Practitioner course that integrates everything flowing into the wiki and presents it as the current state of the art. The community's up-votes distil it, with a way to flag errors, and the commons reviews it; I take part as an ordinary contributor. It updates itself as the wiki changes. Its price is a starting value (D56, PROPOSAL).
- **Curated courses (D57).** Any user can hand-select content into their own course or collection, from the wiki and beyond, for a team, a client or a learning path. Curation is actively encouraged, because many forms of curation bring more diversity and perspectives than any programmatic approach. We compare how well the automatic course and curated collections work (D90, PROPOSAL).
- **The seed (D58).** The Liberating Structures website and my existing deck and videos populate the first version, labelled as a hatch seed. Other practitioners add theirs from day one, and everything is ranked by up-votes.

Course revenue enters the flow, and content a course uses counts as use for its authors; a curator's selection counts too (D59).

## Priority 3: the marketplace

Paid sessions and hiring will happen anyway; the app's job is to make them easy, and to offer to handle billing for practitioners who want that (D67). The marketplace ranks below the commons because the commons is what makes the practice worth buying.

Three things are bought and sold, and each is already partly drawn in draft 3 (D66):

- **Seats in sessions:** the community door (S1), with tier, price and seats.
- **Practitioners for hire:** the practitioner card (S3) and the hiring brief (S4).
- **Certificates of practice:** the formal document of an earned record, to show an employer or an L&D budget holder (see Priority 1). Certificates moved forward: they are pilot 1's one paid path (D128).

## How money flows

Revenue is distributed by threshold-based flow funding, run during the hatch as an explicit experiment (D73, PROPOSAL until the start vote adopts it, D169). Money follows appreciation and use until people's stated needs are met, and the common pool is one of the recipients.

This creates an explicit route to a livelihood through serving the community. I call it a certification pyramid scheme as a joke; the design is the opposite of one.

### Where revenue comes from

The sources are of two kinds: products the commons makes, and transactions between people that the app can carry if they want it to (D74).

| Source | Kind | What is paid for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Courses | Commons product | The online courses generated from the wiki (Priority 2). Full price to the pool (D76) |
| Certificates of practice | Commons product | A formal certificate of an earned record. Full price to the pool (D76) |
| White-label licences | Commons product | Other communities running the protocol, or us running it for them (D104) |
| Paid sessions | Opt-in billing | A levy, only when a practitioner or user group lets the app handle billing (D75) |
| Hiring | Opt-in billing | A levy, only when a hire is billed through the app (D75) |

A practitioner's own fee for their work stays theirs (D77). A community's session split divides that session's own income, shared with a Happy Money Story; only the levy enters the flow (D97). Levy rates are low and visible during the hatch; the commons sets them (D78, OPEN). Everything runs in euros through the Monerium rail (D98); community credit stays open for the commons.

### The common pool

The pool first only fills (D163). Certificate fees and other commons revenue accumulate, and nothing is allocated out until flow funding starts. From day one it is held under a threshold of signers: the three pilot stewards hold its keys, and any two of them sign (D200). Nobody, including me, moves it alone.

Twice a year, at the spring and autumn equinox, stewards can make grants from the pool for stewarding and new practice spaces, shared out with a Happy Money Story (D164, D167). Grants need several signers from several groups, have a cap per equinox, and never go to a signer, their group, their family or anyone paying them (D164, D190). This starts once flow funding has started.

### How it flows

One flow replaces fixed shares. The recipients are the people who opt in and the common pool (D79, PROPOSAL).

**When it starts (D165).** Flow funding starts in two steps. First, the pool holds enough to cover one season of survival thresholds for everyone who has asked for flow funding. Then the next equinox gathering decides whether to start. That vote also adopts the flow rules below, and I abstain from it, as their author. No payout reaches me before it (D169).

1. **Signals (D80).** Two equally weighted signals guide the flow (PROPOSAL): appreciation (Positive Gossip, the session debrief and the traces) and use (a card, string or tip earns when it is used in a co-signed session). Only co-signed sessions pay; claims and imported history show on the record but don't pay. Each member has the same appreciation budget per season, so giving in more sessions spreads it, never adds weight (D183). Until the trust wire ships, sessions outside the pilot community count for appreciation only (D172). PROPOSAL: only sessions announced in advance on a user group's open calendar count for money (D186). OPEN (Mujo): how the records carry appreciation.
2. **Thresholds (D81).** Every recipient states a survival threshold (what they need to get by) and a thriving threshold (what lets them do their best work). Thresholds are self-declared and public, published before each equinox. Anyone can question one, and it is talked through in that equinox's Happy Money Story round. If that doesn't settle it, the recipient's user group votes. PROPOSAL: the commons decides instead (D191). The common pool has thresholds too: its survival threshold is the protocol's upkeep; its thriving threshold covers stewarding and grants for new practice spaces (D82, PROPOSAL).
3. **Flow (D83).** Funds flow toward recipients in proportion to the signals, filling survival thresholds first, then thriving (PROPOSAL). The work happens through the season, and flows are settled at each equinox (D80).
4. **Overflow (D84).** During the hatch there is no overflow: anything above a recipient's thriving threshold goes to the common pool. Overflow to "the people they appreciate" is designed in the parameters conversation that closes the hatch (OPEN: the commons), with no routing to oneself, family or anyone paying the recipient, and no cycles (D196, PROPOSAL).

The common pool as a recipient is how the game stays omni-win: the commons' needs are counted alongside everyone else's, never as an afterthought. Neither the platform nor I take anything outside the flow (D85).

A route to livelihood through community service will create different classes of participants. That is real, and we will have to deal with it rather than pretend it away. Two guards: livelihoods from the flow should be sufficient, not large; and governance follows stewardship recognized by peers (who is trusted, listened to, certifying and practicing), never earnings (D86, D87). The data behind that recognition stays in the data commons. How much is sufficient, and how to read stewardship from the data, are the community's to work out (OPEN).

Paying people is income, so tax applies. OPEN (Mujo and me): whether the platform reports payouts, or each recipient handles their own.

### Rules that keep it honest

- **Pay for contribution, never for recruitment (D168).** Payment follows use of a contribution in a co-signed session, never the number of people adopted, taught, brought in or appreciated. Adopting a home deck and "learned from" lineage carry recognition and phase only. This is the line that separates a commons from an actual pyramid scheme, and it has to hold in code. PROPOSAL (D197): the payout calculation takes only a fixed, published list of inputs, with no field for sign-ups or headcount, and the signers check every line at each equinox before money moves.
- **Opt in (D94).** Nobody's data or earnings are counted without their consent.
- **Visible flows (D95).** Every flow is shown in the open, and its rules change only by the commons, as the S5 pot already does for one session.
- **Equal evidence (D96).** Paid and unpaid sessions produce the same kind of record. A paid session is tagged, not weighted, unless a charter decides otherwise.
- **A small circle before the trust wire.** No money moves in pilot 1: the pool only fills (D163). Once flow funding starts, a circle is held back by one appreciation budget per member (D183), one counted account per person (D184), no pay for use when the author was in the session (D187), and appreciation-only credit for sessions outside the pilot community (D172). PROPOSAL: only announced sessions count for money (D186), and payout inputs are fixed and checked by signers (D197).
- **Risks stated openly (D203).** Even with all of this, a small circle can still co-sign itself to a certificate (it shows how few witnessed it); collusion inside the pilot can't be seen from the network's shape until the trust wire; using someone's tip stays a host's claim; and "one person, one account" depends on NAOMS. We accept these as paint on the road, and we discuss and evaluate them at each equinox.

### Out of scope for now

A token with a bonding curve on the common pool, and a speculative market in the value of these tools and services, is interesting. So is a shared token that pulls many communities of practice onto the protocol: the more practice we certify, the more legitimate we all become. Both wait until the common pool is large enough to be worth experimenting with (D99).

## Governance: stewards, the commons and the hatch

You can found a community, but you cannot own it. This section is how that holds here (feedback §03).

**Stewards (D199, D159).** Any user may ask to be recognised as a steward; requests are evaluated at the equinox. A new steward is elected by unanimous decision of the existing stewards, and stewards come from different user contexts. Any steward, founders included, can be removed by a majority of the group's members who vote. For the pilot, I select and nominate three stewards from the initial group as part of the rollout. I use that power once, it ends when they are seated, and they can be removed like any other steward. I hold no standing seat (D35).

**The commons (D166).** Members vote within their own user group; a decision passes when a majority of participating groups each reach a majority, and stewards from several groups sign it. A group counts in these votes once it has been active for a full season with enough co-signed members (D189). The design follows Ostrom's core design principles for cooperation and the ten Liberating Structures principles. When money is shared among people, we use a Happy Money Story: appreciation, sharing needs, proposals, review, agreement (D167). Rule changes stay with the majorities.

**Founding sealers (D33).** The seal comes in pilot 2. The founding sealers are user groups acting through their stewards, invited by the three pilot stewards, not by me; I am one nominee among others, and the list is public. A seal needs at least three stewards (D159). There is no personal invitation power left to end: mine is limited to nominating the three pilot stewards once (D199).

**Communities join by invitation (D136).** Pilot 1 starts with LS Go Online and the Wise Crowds Design Call together, each joining because its stewards agree. Later communities join by open invitation and are welcomed at the next equinox gathering.

**Starting values (D170).** Everything in this document that is a starting value (the certificate fee, levy rates, the steward floor and the rest) is confirmed by the pilot group at the start of pilot 1, with me abstaining, and can be changed the same way. The flow rules are adopted by the vote that starts flow funding (D169).

**The hatch (D131–D133).** For now we are initializing: catching up with the current reality of the practice, largely by hand. The hatch runs for a fixed span of flow funding and closes at the next equinox gathering, with a hard maximum counted from the start of pilot 1 (D132). When it ends, the commons sets the real parameters in a rigorous conversation, facilitated through the structures themselves (D133).

**Leaving and forking (D177, D178).** A practitioner leaves with their records, phase and certificates; a seal stays valid. A group that disagrees takes the shared content and its members' records, and the commons can release it a share of the pool in proportion to what it paid in.

## Priority 5: white label for other communities of practice

We want to point at the Liberating Structures implementation and say: you could do this for your community of practice too. That comes last in build time but shapes design now (D9).

The pitch only works if the reference implementation works really well. So Priority 5 asks nothing extra of the first release except one discipline: keep everything specific to Liberating Structures in data, not in code (D106). That means:

- **The taxonomy:** the categories and colors (next section).
- **The rubric:** the Development Phases ladder and its thresholds.
- **The vocabulary:** names like RoTI, Positive Gossip and Leave Traces Behind.

Another community should be able to swap all four without a rewrite. There are two routes: a community licenses the protocol and runs it, or we instantiate it for them as a service (D103). Either way the income goes to the common pool, which also pays for maintaining the protocol (D104).

My long-term hope is an open facilitation toolkit: Deep Democracy, Appreciative Inquiry, sociocracy and agile practices recorded in one way, with shared learning across all of them. A collaboration with SessionLab, which helps facilitators plan sessions, is worth exploring later (D105).

### The knobs on interaction cards

Group and time cards have dials, and a session record should capture their settings (D110):

- **Group size:** solo, pair, trio, four, and larger.

- **Minutes per person per question:** from tight to spacious, or unbounded, taking as long as the context allows.

Minutes per person per question is the deck's own unit for time, and it is comparable across group sizes (D111).
