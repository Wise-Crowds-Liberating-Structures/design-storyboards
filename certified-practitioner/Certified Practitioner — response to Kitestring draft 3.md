# Certified Practitioner — response to Kitestring draft 3

Sep 29, 2026 · @Javeline

## Why we are doing this

We are building new senses for groups, so that what they learn to see, they can learn to value. That is the pitch, and everything below serves it.

The tools we think with become part of us. A hammer used well feels like an extension of the hand, because the brain's model of the body absorbs it. Meditation and mindfulness work the same way on individual thinking.

Liberating Structures take that move collective. A group using them thinks about how it wants to think, together. We call this process mindfulness. The arc runs from mechanical to mystical: first you learn the game rules, then the games become how you see and move through the world.

The structures come out of complexity science, by way of the Santa Fe Institute and narrative inquiry. Ecocycle, the Agreement–Certainty Matrix and many others turn that theory into procedure. Playing them builds a felt, intuitive sense of how complex systems behave. We practice the theory rather than only reading it.

This matters now. We face a pile-up of second-order crises, and many people feel powerless because they meet them with old cognitive tools. The cornerstone of new tools is relationships made legible. What gets perceived can be accounted for. A materialist, reductionist accounting cannot see relationships, so it cannot value them. New senses make a new accounting possible.

In the meantime, the same tools help people get real work done: genuine co-creation that includes and unleashes every voice. Certified Practitioner is how we make that practice visible, trustworthy and self-funding.

## What we are building

Certified Practitioner is a community-owned certification program: it makes practice visible, sells what that visibility is worth, and pays the people who make it valuable. Certified Practitioner is also the name of the app, replacing Kitestring as the working name, and the Liberating Structures community is the first place it runs.

Draft 3 frames the app as a tool for my deck. That frame is too small. The deck and the app are the ingestion layer of something larger: a certification program with a knowledge commons and courses built on top of it, and money designed in from the start.

We are a large organization that is not organized. Today nobody can say where the practice is active, which user groups are running, or where the knowledge sits without someone maintaining lists by hand. Certified Practitioner lets us federate into a trench-coat totem pole: enough coherence and legibility to pass as a large organization to the enterprises that buy learning, while staying a community underneath.

The stars are the user groups: the people already using these tools wherever practice happens, who will use the app to certify their practice.

Five goals, in priority order:

1. **Certified practice.** Practitioners and practice sessions carry certification a community stands behind.
2. **A knowledge commons and the automatic course.** A living wiki of the structures, how they are used and remixed, with Tips, Traps and Riffs, and online courses generated from it.
3. **A marketplace.** Paid sessions and hiring, with the app offering to handle billing for those who want it.
4. **The network sees itself.** The state of the practice is always current and at everyone's fingertips, with no directory upkeep.
5. **A white-label protocol.** Other communities of practice can run the same thing, pointing at ours as the working example, with licence income feeding the common pool.

These stack like a permaculture guild: each goal feeds the others. The ranking says what we protect when they compete for build time.

## Must-have: my deck first, my archive in

When I use the app, it uses my stuff first, every time, no matter what the community's votes say. This is non-negotiable for me as the first practitioner on the platform, and it must not get lost while we work out the marketplace and the money.

### My deck first

Every practitioner has a home deck. Mine is my own cards. Wherever the app shows cards or content, my home deck comes first, and the community's material follows under "also from the community":

- **Find the card (screen 7) and the string builder:** my cards first, then core Liberating Structures, then community riffs.
- **Card recognition:** my card photos are the reference images the camera matches first.
- **Wiki and course views:** my Tips, Traps, Riffs and videos lead on each pattern; community entries follow, ranked by votes.
- **My sessions:** when I run a session, it runs on my deck, and every participant sees my cards first.

Votes rank the community's material; they never reorder my deck in my view or in my sessions. Every practitioner gets the same right for their own deck, so this is a feature of the platform, not a privilege for me. It is also the white-label idea at the scale of one person.

### My archive in

The hatch starts with importing what I already have, so the app is full of my material on day one. The machine does the sorting; I approve what it proposes.

| Source | What it becomes | How it gets in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| YouTube: live session recordings | Past sessions: the string played, transcript, traces | Transcript from captions or the app's transcription; the string and patterns extracted and proposed for my approval |
| YouTube: explainer videos | Course modules and wiki entries per pattern | Tagged by pattern; Tips, Traps and Riffs drafted from the transcript |
| YouTube: design walkthroughs | Designs (strings) with their reasoning | The string and the "why" behind each choice extracted from the transcript |
| Design briefs (Google Docs) | Set + Setting briefs, each linked to its string | Bulk export from Google Drive; fields mapped to Place, Problem, Purpose, Participants, Practical |
| Other Google Docs | Wiki entries and course material | Bulk export; tagged by pattern; entries drafted for review |
| GoodNotes handwritten notes | Wiki entries, with the original page attached | Exported as PDF or JPEG; handwriting read into text; the page image kept beside it |
| Images: card photos | My home deck and the camera's reference images | Matched to cards and categories |
| Images: walls, boards, sketches | Traces attached to sessions and designs | Matched to a session or design by date and content, or left in a library |

Three rules for the import:

- **A review queue, not a dump.** Everything lands as a proposal: pattern tags, extracted strings, drafted Tips, Traps and Riffs. I approve, fix or discard. This is the manual work the hatch expects.
- **Mine is published under my name.** What I authored goes into the wiki attributed to me, and my deck becomes an offering others can adopt as their home deck, with use in their sessions crediting me through the flow.
- **Other people's faces and words stay private.** Session recordings show participants who never agreed to a wiki. Those recordings stay in my private archive; the design and my own notes can be published, and the rest waits for their consent. Past sessions are marked "imported, not co-signed" until someone who was there co-signs.

## Priority 1: certified practice

Certification applies to two things, not one: the practitioner and the practice session. Draft 3 covers the first. The second is new, and it is where most of the market value sits.

User groups already certify people tacitly. When a group has watched you host for a year, it knows what you can do. Certified Practitioner makes that judgment explicit and portable. I spent years in a user group wanting to go pro; this is the path I did not have.

### Certified practitioners

The criteria are shared by everyone; the seal belongs to each user group.

- **One shared rubric.** The Development Phases ladder, read across four dimensions: patterns in use, people reached, diversity of settings, and design goals achieved. A 16+ in Utrecht means the same as a 16+ in Rotterdam.
- **Thresholds.** Despairing Cynic below 8 patterns in use; Cautious Optimist at 8+; Rapturous Super-User at 16+; Maestro Minimalist at 32+. Anyone beyond that is simply a maestro.
- **No ceiling on the deck.** Every pattern card counts, and the deck is not captive to the 43 structures in the 2026 book. Community cards are mostly versions of the 43; a genuinely new card counts once it has been through a development cycle and is in broad use. New structures can be created and added at any time.
- **Computed, not filled in.** Each pattern moves through its own ecocycle for each person: Gestation (never seen it), Birth (led through it), Maturity (hosted others in it), Creative Destruction (enabled someone else to host it). The app's records show which stage applies. "Patterns in use" is the count at Maturity or beyond.
- **Sealed locally.** A user group's stewards attest that the record meets the rubric. Their attestation is the certification, and certifying a record is always free.
- **Bootstrapped by reputation.** During the hatch phase (see Min specs and build order), sealing is an honest oligarchy: user groups and people with established reputation in the community, invited by me through an invitation flow in the app. Records will be thin early on, so sealing must be careful: no seal without a robust record.

### Certified practice sessions

Whether to offer paid sessions, and at what price, is up to user groups and practitioners. The market decides; the session record simply carries its tier so buyers can tell what they are paying for.

| Session tier | Who runs it | What participants get | Reference price |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Community practice | Any user group member | Practice, proof of participation | Usually free, or costs shared |
| Certified practice | A certified practitioner | Practice with an expert, attested learning | Set by the practitioner or user group |
| Immersion workshop | Certified practitioners, explicitly teaching | A structured learning path toward the next phase | Today about €500 per participant per day in the EU |

An immersion day with 20 participants is roughly €10,000 of revenue. Today that flows only to the few practitioners with enough personal brand to fill a room. Certification lets the community's legitimacy fill rooms for practitioners who have the skill but not the brand. What these sessions are worth is a question the community of practice should explore together, openly and over time.

## Priority 2: the knowledge commons

Every recorded session should leave something in a shared wiki, the community should decide what rises, and online courses should fall out of it automatically. The wiki becomes the new reference for the structures, and the courses are our second product line.

### What it holds

- **Per pattern:** Tips (what makes it come together), Traps (what to avoid, and how), and Riffs (a remix of one element for a narrow use).
- **Per string:** designs that were actually played, with their outcomes.
- **Per setting:** which patterns are used for which problems, where, and where the practice is innovating.
- **The cards themselves.** Three layers, shown side by side: the 43 core Liberating Structures as published; my deck, offered as one community set among many (this is my stuff, check it out, make your own); and community riffs on either. Any practitioner can create a card, offer it through the app, and have the community evaluate it, so the deck keeps evolving. I hope others make far better cards than mine, including better versions of the ones I made. Other published decks, such as Holisticon's, could join the same way later. The app needs an affordance for offering a card as a revision of an existing one, with versions, so people can see the alternatives, choose between them, and customize their own deck.

### How it is sorted

Like Reddit: up and down votes, so the cream rises. Ostrom's principles are the governance frame: the community can see what is in the commons and rate it, so it governs itself.

Votes rank the wiki but never pay. Recognition money follows use: an entry earns when it is used in a co-signed session, which is far harder to fake than a vote.

### How entries get in

A new step, Data Wash, comes after the session closes. Each contributor chooses, per entry, to publish it under their name, publish it anonymized, or withhold it. This is build item 16, sized S: a filter and a feed over data that items 1–5 already produce.

The closing order becomes: traces, then RoTI, then Positive Gossip, then Data Wash. Evidence comes first, then its evaluation, then the co-signature, then the choice to publish.

### The wiki-to-course pipeline

The pipeline is in scope, and its automation is the point: courses are a byproduct, not a separate production effort.

- **The automatic course.** One official Certified Practitioner course that integrates everything flowing into the wiki and presents it as the current state of the art. It updates itself as the wiki changes, and costs about €50.
- **Curated courses.** Any user can hand-select wiki content into their own course, for a team, a client or a learning path.
- **The seed.** The Liberating Structures website and my existing videos populate the first version, so the course is useful before the wiki is full.

Course revenue enters the flow, and content a course uses counts as use for its authors.

### Licence

The Liberating Structures material is licensed [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://www.liberatingstructures.com/core-ls-books), covering both core books including the 2026 Fieldbook by Keith McCandless and Nancy White. There is no non-commercial term, so selling courses built on it is allowed.

Two consequences follow. ShareAlike means wiki and course content built on the material carries the same licence, so anyone may reuse it; people pay for curation, live updates and certification, not exclusive content. And the LS site asks that the core assets stay constant: the ten principles, the 43 icons, and the min specs of the 43 structures. New cards and structures in our deck should be marked as community riffs, distinct from that core.

### Your session, as context for your agents

The data a session produces belongs to the people who made it. The app should let anyone download one package from a meeting holding everyone's permitted notes, transcripts and photos, ready to hand to their own AI agents as shared context, for example as an MCP server. This is a strong value-first offer: record with us, and your agents know what your group knows.

## Priority 3: the marketplace

Paid sessions and hiring will happen anyway; the app's job is to make them easy, and to offer to handle billing for practitioners who want that. The marketplace ranks below the commons because the commons is what makes the practice worth buying.

Three things are bought and sold, and each is already partly drawn in draft 3:

- **Seats in sessions** — the community door (S1), with tier, price and seats.
- **Practitioners for hire** — the practitioner card (S3) and the hiring brief (S4).
- **Diplomas** — a formal certificate of a sealed practice, to show an employer or an L&D budget holder. Like requesting a diploma from your school, the certificate costs a fee (about €100 during the hatch); the certification behind it does not. Certification is relational, so the diploma is signed by Certified Practitioner and the peers who witnessed the practice, and names those relations. A training's fee includes certification, so every training sends money to the commons.

What is never for sale is the claim. Money buys learning, practice and a formal document of an earned record. It cannot buy a record that was not earned.

## How money flows

All revenue is distributed by threshold-based flow funding, run during the hatch as an explicit experiment. Money follows appreciation and use until people's stated needs are met, and the common pool is one of the recipients.

This creates an explicit route to a livelihood through serving the community. I call it a certification pyramid scheme as a joke; the design is the opposite of one.

### Where revenue comes from

The sources are of two kinds: products the commons makes, and transactions between people that the app can carry if they want it to.

| Source | Kind | What is paid for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Courses | Commons product | The online courses generated from the wiki (Priority 2) |
| Diplomas | Commons product | A formal certificate of a sealed practice, for an employer or budget holder |
| White-label licences | Commons product | Other communities running the protocol, or us running it for them |
| Paid sessions | Opt-in billing | A levy, only when a practitioner or user group lets the app handle billing |
| Hiring | Opt-in billing | A levy, only when a hire is billed through the app |

A practitioner's own fee for their work stays theirs.

### How it flows

One flow replaces fixed shares. The recipients are the people who opt in and the common pool.

1. **Signals.** Two equally weighted signals guide the flow: appreciation (Positive Gossip, the session debrief and the traces) and use (a card, string or tip earns when it is used in a co-signed session). Votes rank the wiki but never pay.
2. **Thresholds.** Every recipient states a survival threshold (what they need to get by) and a thriving threshold (what lets them do their best work). Thresholds are self-declared and public, with no review during the hatch. The common pool has thresholds too: its survival threshold is the protocol's upkeep; its thriving threshold covers stewarding and grants for new practice spaces.
3. **Flow.** Funds flow toward recipients in proportion to the signals, filling survival thresholds first, then thriving.
4. **Overflow.** Once a recipient reaches thriving, anything more flows on to the people they appreciate. What overflows everywhere settles in the common pool.

The common pool as a recipient is how the game stays omni-win: the commons' needs are counted alongside everyone else's, never as an afterthought. Neither the platform nor I take anything outside the flow.

A route to livelihood through community service will create different classes of participants. That is real, and we will have to deal with it rather than pretend it away. Two guards: livelihoods from the flow should be sufficient, not large; and governance follows stewardship recognized by peers (who is trusted, listened to, certifying and practicing), never earnings. The data behind that recognition stays in the data commons.

### The experiment

In Strategyzer Test Card form. The targets are proposals for the hatch, to be confirmed.

| Step |  |
| --- | --- |
| We believe that | Threshold-based flow funding, guided by appreciation and use, will get active practitioners to their survival threshold more fairly than a fixed formula, while keeping the common pool funded |
| To verify that, we will | Route all hatch-phase revenue through flow funding for the founding participants for at least 13 months, and publish every flow |
| And measure | Share of participants at survival and at thriving; months to reach survival; the common pool's coverage of protocol upkeep; share of funds going to the top 10% of recipients; participants' own rating of fairness; retention of participants |
| We are right if | Most participants rate the flow fair; the common pool covers protocol upkeep; no small group captures most of the funds; participants stay |
| If we are wrong, we will | Fall back to fixed thirds (contribution, recognition, common pool) and bring what we learned into the parameters conversation |

### Rules that keep it honest

- **Pay for contribution, never for recruitment.** No one earns from signing up someone else. This is the line that separates a commons from an actual pyramid scheme, and it has to hold in code.
- **Opt in.** Nobody's data or earnings are counted without their consent.
- **Visible flows.** Every flow is shown in the open, and its rules change only by the commons, as the S5 pot already does for one session.
- **Equal evidence.** Paid and unpaid sessions produce the same kind of record. A paid session is tagged, not weighted, unless a charter decides otherwise.

### Out of scope for now

A token with a bonding curve on the common pool, and a speculative market in the value of these tools and services, is interesting. So is a shared token that pulls many communities of practice onto the protocol: the more practice we certify, the more legitimate we all become. Both wait until the common pool is large enough to be worth experimenting with.

## Priority 4: the network sees itself

The community-wide view should fall out of the records, so nobody ever maintains a user-group list by hand again. Draft 3 calls this landscape useful but not needed; I would promote it to a public dashboard.

It answers the questions we cannot answer today: where the practice is active, which user groups are running, which structures are used for which problems, what is trending, where it is being invited in, and where the leading edge is growing. A system that can see itself can grow, change and evolve. That same view is what makes us legible to an enterprise buyer.

| Metric | Definition | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time to record | Median minutes from first card scan to session closed | Proves or disproves "nobody types a record" |
| Gossip completion | Share of players who send Positive Gossip within 24 hours | The co-signature is the evidence; without it nothing downstream holds |
| Witness diversity | Median number of distinct communities in a practitioner's witnesses | Tracks how hard the evidence is to fake |
| Certification funnel | Requested, reviewed, sealed or "not yet", with median days per step | Shows whether "not yet, warmly" works or becomes a bottleneck |
| Repeat sessions | Share of communities logging a second session within 30 days | Whether recording helps or gets in the way |
| Data Wash uptake | Share of outputs published attributed, anonymized or withheld | Makes the publishing choice visible as a pattern |
| Journal activity | New entries per 10 sessions, and votes per entry per week | Whether the commons is growing and being governed |
| Revenue and flow | Monthly euros in, and share of recipients at survival and at thriving | Whether the practice is becoming a living |

Witness diversity and anything that crosses communities cannot be measured until the trust wire (M0) ships. The dashboard should say so plainly rather than show a flat zero.

## Priority 5: white label for other communities of practice

We want to point at the Liberating Structures implementation and say: you could do this for your community of practice too. That comes last in build time but shapes design now.

The pitch only works if the reference implementation works really well. So Priority 5 asks nothing extra of the first release except one discipline: keep everything specific to Liberating Structures in data, not in code. That means:

- **The deck** — the card catalogue and its photos.
- **The taxonomy** — the categories and colors (next section).
- **The rubric** — the Development Phases ladder and its thresholds.
- **The vocabulary** — names like RoTI, Positive Gossip and Leave Traces Behind.

Another community should be able to swap all four without a rewrite. There are two routes: a community licenses the protocol and runs it, or we instantiate it for them as a service. Either way the income goes to the common pool, which also pays for maintaining the protocol.

My long-term hope, said in the Learning Journal design years ago, is an open facilitation toolkit: Deep Democracy, Appreciative Inquiry, sociocracy and agile practices recorded in one way, with shared learning across all of them. A collaboration with SessionLab, which helps facilitators plan sessions, is worth exploring later.

## The deck as the data model

The deck's colors are a taxonomy of how the structures are used and related, and they map onto the five design elements of every Liberating Structure. If the app records which category each card belongs to and the setting of its knobs, every session record becomes the design made explicit.

| Color | Category | What it encodes | Design element |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dark green | Meta-games: situational assessment | Seeing and sensing the group and its situation | Purpose and context |
| Light green | Meta-games: design and deliver | Designing the intervention and delivering it | Purpose and context |
| Purple | Artifacts: maps and traces | Maps orient a conversation; traces leave a record of where the group was, what it thought and decided | Materials and space |
| Light pink | Question compass | Questions that point a conversation in a direction | Invitation |
| Pink | Question strings | Sequences of questions, such as Discovery & Action Dialogue and Purpose to Practice | Invitation |
| Blue (roles) | Interaction: roles | Rights, permissions and modes of participating, such as Scribe, Time Lord and Vibe Canary | Distributing participation |
| Blue (groups, steps and time) | Interaction: configuration | How large the groups are and how time is spent | Groups; steps and time |

The deck is a starting place: new cards enter through the wiki (Priority 2), so these categories will fill and may change. This answers draft 3's open question 7. The two purple families are **maps** and **traces**.

### The question compass

The light pink cards sit on a compass. North asks for more, south for less, west looks to the past, east to the future.

|  | Past |  | Future |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **More** | Appreciative Interviews | Wicked Questions | 15% Solutions |
|  | 9 Whys |  | What, So What, Now What |
| **Less** | Heard, Seen, Respected | Min Specs | TRIZ (Creative Destruction) |

This gives "find the card" (screen 7) a second way in: someone can ask for a question that points toward the future and toward less, and get TRIZ.

### The knobs on interaction cards

Group and time cards have dials, and a session record should capture their settings:

- **Group size:** solo, pair, trio, four, and larger.
- **Group stability:** stable groups, or groups that shuffle.
- **Minutes per person per question:** 1 minute at the tight end, about 10 at the spacious end, or unbounded, taking as long as the context allows.

That last dial replaces draft 4's "storyboard minutes". Minutes per person per question is the deck's own unit, and it is comparable across group sizes.

### The paper needs a pass too

PSLP v2.1 is now out of date in three places, and draft 4 should not inherit them.

- **§3.6.3, pay-to-certify.** The model is now: certifying a record is free; a fee applies only to a diploma, the formal certificate of an earned record.
- **§3.6.2, the Creative Commons argument.** It rests on the old non-commercial licence. Under Attribution-ShareAlike, the case for the protocol is no longer that selling is forbidden, but that the claim is never for sale.
- **§3.6.5, the trust gap.** It says your §07 finding "resolved" the gap. It located the gap in code. Until the trust wire ships, yes-or-no recognition between communities is the honest floor.

The ladder also changes: thresholds of 8, 16 and 32 patterns in use replace the card's 5, 15+ and 33, and the deck has no ceiling.

## Min specs and build order

Run through Min Specs, the whole design reduces to six musts and five must-nots. Anything not on these lists is negotiable.

### Musts

1. A real session is recorded with no typing and co-signed by gossip.
2. Credentials become trust edges (the §07 wire).
3. A practitioner's phase is computed from their records.
4. A user group seals one practitioner against the shared rubric, and warmly declines another.
5. A paid session or a diploma runs through the app, and every flow of its money is visible.
6. Contributors choose how their entries reach the wiki, the community's votes sort it, and a course generates itself from it.

### Must-nots

- Never sell the claim; only learning, practice and the reading of an earned record.
- Never pay anyone for recruiting someone else.
- Never take a share for the platform or the deck author outside what the commons decides.
- Never show a number the trust wire cannot yet support.
- Never hardcode what is specific to Liberating Structures.

### Build order

We start with an explicit hatch phase. The build follows your milestones, with two additions and one pilot.

**The hatch.** For now we are initializing: catching up with the current reality of the practice, largely by hand, to populate the system. Parameters in this document (the flow-funding experiment, the founding oligarchy, the levy rates) are starting values, not decisions. The hatch lasts at least 13 months, the length of the flow-funding experiment. When it ends, the real parameters are set in a rigorous community conversation, facilitated through the structures themselves.

1. **M0 · Trust wire and M1 · Play and record,** in parallel, as proposed. We have real sessions recorded already; M1 means sessions recorded through the app, including my deck first, my imported archive, and the downloadable session package for agents.
2. **M2 · Players and privacy,** plus Data Wash (build 16).
3. **Pilot** in the wild, starting with the Wise Crowds Design Call as the most frequent channel, with founding sealers I invite. The LS Go Online user group is the next candidate.
4. **M3 · Practice and certification,** with the shared rubric, the computed ladder, OGD Eval on designs, session tiers, and diplomas.
5. **M4 · The commons,** with the journal view, voting, card submissions and versions, the wiki-to-course pipeline seeded from the website and my videos, and the network dashboard.
6. **M5 · A living,** with opt-in billing, the levy, and the flow-funding experiment.
7. **The parameters conversation,** closing the hatch with the experiment's results.
8. **Later:** white-label packaging, then token experiments once the common pool is large.

The order follows the priorities: certification first, then the commons and its courses, then the marketplace. There is nothing to sell until records exist and a community can seal them.

## Recommendation: the absolute minimum

Cut to the bone, Certified Practitioner is my deck and archive plus four things: a trustworthy record of practice, reputation computed from it, certification sealed by a user group, and a wiki that writes its own course. Everything else waits until the pilot proves those four work.
