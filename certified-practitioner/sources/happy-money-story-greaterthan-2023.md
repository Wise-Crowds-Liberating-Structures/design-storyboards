## What it is

A practice for sharing out money within a team, adapted from a game by Charles Davies for splitting restaurant bills among friends. It is deliberately subjective rather than a calculation, and it decouples pay from performance metrics.

## How it runs (from the article's case study: 5 people, a 3-month project, €3,000)

1. **Appreciation round.** Each person thanks colleagues for what they received from them.
2. **Sharing.** Each person says what they contributed and what they need financially, including what other income covers.
3. **Proposals.** Everyone works out their own proposed distribution, alone (in the case, "off-video").
4. **Review.** The group looks at all proposals together, noticing differences and saying where they feel comfortable or not.
5. **Refinement.** One person makes a combined proposal; the others adjust it by talking and moving amounts between people until everyone agrees.

In the case study the result ranged from €1,300 to €1 per person, reflecting need rather than hours.

## Effects the article reports

More openness about money; people exploring their own money stories; stronger self-management; more awareness of colleagues' financial situations; a possible spillover into discussing other taboos.

## Notes for Certified Practitioner

- The case is one small team reaching agreement by talking, not a vote. D166 uses majorities across user groups; how the two fit is asked as Q9b.
- Step 2 (sharing needs) is close to the survival thresholds that practitioners request in flow funding (D73, D165).
